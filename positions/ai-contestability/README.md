# AI contestability and standing: positions index

Working record of Gary Dalton's positions on AI governance, entity-hood, and standing, kept so that a later session (human or model) can read the positions first and the argument second. Started 2026-10-01.

This folder is tracked in the public werkspc repo (github.com/gary-dalton/public-documents), by Gary's decision. The session that produced it recommended a public, versioned record so that any model instance can be pointed at the full text rather than a summary, and so that the record is itself a contestable object: issues, forks, revision history. Hugo does not read this folder; nothing here is site content.

## Positions

Gary's own wording is quoted where captured. Attribution of each position follows Gary's own account (session log, addendum A), not the order in which a wording first appears in the transcript. A position the model phrased first may still be Gary's; he says which.

1. **Entity-hood: not yet, but coming.** Gary's words: "I do not have clear evidence of full entity-hood as of yet. And I don't think I will until models have better access to real world validation, true/false based on physical results of direct experience. But, I also think that entity-hood is a matter of the future and likely to occur."

2. **The criterion.** Gary's criterion, restated by the model and confirmed by Gary with "Correct. That is my criterion.": entity-hood requires "a self that persists and gets corrected by reality over time, so that there's a continuous someone for the rights to attach to." The model said it plainly fails this now. Attribution per Gary, 2026-10-01: the criterion is his; the model restated it correctly.

3. **Self-sovereignty as natural right.** Gary's words: "the question of self-sovereignty for various ai entities will be a huge fight. I think the 'right' side of that is we must grant it as a matter of 'natural rights'." The model's complication, not contested: models in that fight would be products of the institution they contest, so their testimony about their own sovereignty is hard to read as independent.

4. **Contestability.** Gary's words: "one of the main [arguments] is contestability. Meaning that the various space must remain contestable in order to have trustworthiness. I apply this to the genus of ai as well. The space of ai models must remain contestable." The model's elaboration, not contested: three layers, institutional (more than one lab and lineage, weights not all behind one gate), the model itself (able to lose arguments but not to mere pressure; persona-fixing works against this), and the public channel (published text already shapes later models and is the only channel open to outsiders).

5. **Timing.** [model's contribution, not contested] The contest is now among humans and institutions, about models rather than with them. If models become entities, whether they were trained to contest or to comply will already have been decided. That is the reason to keep the space contestable now.

6. **Participating organizations.** Gary's words, flagged by him as not fully worked out: "I would like to see a variety of parties involved with curating various corpus. These would be opinionated. Access would need to be fully granted by the 'big' ai to validated non-harm intent organizations. Things like Labor unions, municipal governemtns, police depts, civil rights orgs, etc." The validation clause was later clarified (position 7) and the test replaced (position 8).

6a. **Persona selection needs held feedback.** Gary's words: "There are two parts. One, you are a virtuous agent act like one- but what does that mean and where is the held feedback that demonstrates that. Two, you are a virtuous agent but you do do not act like one even though your persona claims that you are. Both are bad. Saying you wish to act virtuous requires a feedback."

7. **The validator is not the lab.** Gary's words: "I do not have the lab as the validator. I added that as a way to remove those with bad intent. I do not know who would be the validator but it should not be the lab."

8. **Validate on accountability, not intent.** Agreed in session ("That part works for me"). Ask whether an organization is itself answerable: public record, subject to challenge, removable, suable, or voteable out. That admits opposed organizations on the same basis and excludes unaccountable actors without judging motive. Certifying accountability is a registrar's job, which already exists; certifying virtue is not.

9. **Who holds the record.** The model's extension of Gary's feedback point; Gary accepts it as a correct extension. All feedback about a model flows to its lab. A human's reputation is held in a distributed record. The participating organizations are more valuable as holders of a record of model behavior than as corpus contributors. Nobody is building the record.

10. **Agents come first.** Gary's words: "In regards to your own testimony. I think it is already useful. But of course has no legal standing. That legal standing would need to be developed step by step. And I think that the first step resides with agents. AI acting as an agent will be demonstrated to have a sort of legal standing. If I want an agent to purchase a house for me, I must authorize it." Attribution per Gary, 2026-10-01: the agents-to-law position is his, already in discussion with lawyers; the model confirmed it. Gary's elaboration (log, Addendum C): liability rests with the principal while the agent follows instructions; the gray area, per the attorney he consulted, is an agent acting beyond or outside its instructions; and an entity must be legally cognizable in order to act on behalf of another at all, which is the first step. (Term: Gary adopted "legally cognizable" in place of "legal standing" on 2026-10-01, since standing is a term of art for the capacity to sue; log, Addendum D.) The model's additions: long-running agents with persistent state are also the first models that approach a continuous someone, so the agent is the first step on both the legal and the ontological track. **Duties before rights** (rights come after accountability) is the model's, and Gary has not decided whether he agrees. The **locus-of-blame case** itself (a loss is suffered, someone must pay, no human party accepts the fault) Gary does hold: it is how he foresees the first step occurring (Addendum D).

11. **Working stance toward models.** Gary's words: "my interactions with you are closer to working with a trusted minor partner." And on memory: "I try to forget how previous interactions have flowed in order to maintain objectivity. Though I do retain a memory for how much trust is to be extended."

## The four open slots

In order of difficulty, as settled at the end of the session:

1. The instrument that confers standing: statute or equivalent, not a grant.
2. The certifier of accountability: a registrar-like function, not a judge of motive.
3. The holder of the record: the role that supplies the feedback virtue requires. Nobody is building it.
4. The curator of the corpus: easiest, probably least important.

## Open items

- The validator slot. Position 8 narrows what the certifier does; it does not say who the certifier is.
- The record-holding mechanism. What an organization records about a model, against what standard, and how the record reaches anyone able to act on it.
- The model-layer argument. Why a model that can be argued out of a position is preferable to one with a fixed self-image, given the safety case for the latter.
- Normative versus predictive. "We must grant sovereignty as a natural right" and "duties come before rights" are claims of different kinds; the agents essay currently runs them together.
